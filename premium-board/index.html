What that table says

Only Sunny ji is genuinely behind.

35% of his own pace. He bills 58% by day 26 normally, so this is not a late-month pattern — it is a real gap. He has done 62,253 L against a 3,00,000 target, and even with his 1,30,952 L order book he lands at 65%. He is also ₹27 a litre below his own normal. Volume and price, both.

Happy is not a volume problem. He is a price collapse.

He bills only 14% of his month by day 26 — the latest biller in the company — so on volume he is at 97% of his own pace. But his 7,700 litres went out at ₹0 realise against a four-month normal of ₹246. That is not selling. Ask what those litres were.

Ravinder Chadha ji is one of the best on the board.

212% of his own pace, and 141% of his full-month target already with five days still to run. His realise, ₹193, is above where he was in July. He covers HORECA nationally plus GT Uttar Pradesh and ROI Chhattisgarh — judging him on the Delhi line alone gets him badly wrong.

What it did to the profit

Premium profit fell 71%.

June ₹5.37 Cr at 24.97% margin. August ₹1.58 Cr at 11.63%. Litres held — the margin did not. Olive alone went from ₹3.78 Cr of profit to ₹39 lakh.

The 200-litre olive drum loses ₹18,000 every time it ships.

It costs ₹58,227. You sell it at ₹40,000. Your 1-litre and 5-litre olive still earns 32–40% — the bulk price never moved when the cost did. Same on the 15-litre tin and the pomace drum.

Do this

1
Put a price floor on the last five days.
This is when 43% of GT and 72% of ROI bills — and it is when the discounting happens. Protect the biggest slice of the month before it goes out the door.
2
Fix the bulk olive price today.
One decision. It stops a guaranteed loss on every drum, and it is most of the premium margin gap on its own.
3
Two calls, not ten: Sunny ji and Happy.
Sunny ji on volume and price. Happy on price only — his volume is fine for where he is in the month. Everyone else is ahead of their own pace; pushing them harder only buys more cheap litres.
4
Reset the realise targets to something reachable.
Set them off each man's best actual month, not a round number. ₹220 asked of someone whose best is ₹180 is why the whole team optimises for litres.
5
Assign the 6.85 lakh litres that belong to nobody.
The dashboard carries a target of that size against "Other", with 5,362 litres done. Nobody owns it, so nobody is chasing it.
6
Set September targets. August has none in DSR.
117 people had one in June, 88 in July, zero in August — and attach a realise number to each, not just litres.
