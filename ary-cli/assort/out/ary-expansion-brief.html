The shelf, not the range

This review was commissioned to find every product the ~4,700 people at Baru Sahib need that ARY does not sell. Twenty-one categories were sized against the live books. Eighteen came back saying the same thing: the range is fine, the shelf is empty.

01 · The finding

ARY is not short of things to sell. It is short of the things it already sells.

The brief assumed a missing-category problem — no pharmacy, no baby aisle, no hardware. Some of that is true and small. The large number is not a category at all.

04 · What the verify pass killed

Six things this review published, and had to withdraw

No research lane in this exercise had ever been adversarially checked. When fifteen were, 106 of 293 claims failed — 91 of them load-bearing, and seven of the fifteen lanes now rate too low to act on. What follows was all previously asserted here as fact.

❌ The ₹2.51 crore institutional food opportunity does not exist

Eternal University's "₹1.96 Cr mess meal charges" is a cooked-meal transfer charge — its own statement books "consumable items ₹22.50 lakh" as a separate line. The ₹55 L of staff boarding is exactly offset by ₹55 L of staff collections. And ₹15.06 L of the "₹49.47 L ARY already captures" is booked on the clothing counter. Both sides of the ratio were the wrong thing. Even the campus mess buys juice, chocolate and aprons from ARY — no rice, atta or dal above ₹3,000.

❌ FusionERP8 cannot store batch and expiry — the pharmacy blocker is real

This was published as solved. It is not. ProductChildMaster holds 111,253 rows and zero real expiry dates — every one is the 1900-01-01 sentinel. Only the "Food Products" template defines Batch/Mfg/Exp fields, and it covers 49 SKUs (0.25%); "General Products" covers 19,054 of 19,494 and defines none of them. The 22 "batches" cited as proof are nine years of price revisions, one per cost change, never two live at once.

❌ Gross margin is 24.4% on retail, not the 31% every forecast used

The 22% figure circulated later was itself an artefact — sales minus purchases, which is not cost of goods sold. ClosingStock has zero rows: no year has ever been closed in this database. On purchase-ledger weighted-average cost the real figures are retail 24.42%, named accounts 26.52%, wholesale 1.04%.

❌ The BIS appliance deadline was overstated, and its escape hatch is not ARY's

Room heaters, electric irons, hair dryers and fans are not named in the order's schedule — that is ₹1.13 L of the ₹2.82 L outside it entirely. The six-month sell-down declaration belongs to the manufacturer or importer holding the licence; a retailer in Baru Sahib cannot file it. And the penalty gloss was inverted: ₹2 lakh is a statutory floor, with the real ceiling at ten times goods value.

❌ The infant-formula exposure is an empty rack; the live one is drugs

All nine formula SKUs hold zero stock; seven have never sold a line in the system's history and the other two last sold in August 2024. The six-month-minimum sentence was attached to four units of Cerelac. The genuinely live, actively restocked exposure is allopathic drugs: 45 active SKUs, ₹39,672 across 613 bills, including a Schedule H prescription drug. The published remedy named three brands and closed 18% of it by value while reporting the offence closed.

❌ Seven categories were "added" that ARY already stocks

Each was called new by a name-match that missed the shelf: toys 421 SKUs / ₹4.65 L, electricals 312 SKUs, insecticides ₹32,753, Mamaearth 16 SKUs / ₹36,983 (the search used one word; the shop files it "Mama Earth"), intimate wash (V Wash, 82 units a year), the ₹99–199 sunscreen rung — already the largest unit band — and Korean noodles at a counter that never listed them. This is why "missing" carries the burden of proof here, never "covered".

05 · What to do

Four moves, and none of them is buying stock

Ordered so nothing expensive happens before the question it depends on is answered. Every category-add in this review is invalid until the first one holds.

1

Put a reorder point on the 629 shelf-stable sellers that are empty

Recoverable gross profit ₹4–9 lakh — estimated, because nothing in this data measures how much demand substitutes to a sibling SKU rather than walking out. The whole twelve-month buy for these lines already sits inside existing purchase volume, so this needs no capital. Korean noodles sold out in three weeks of April and were not reordered for 84 days, in the store's largest month on record. 06 · The discipline

What not to do — and why each one is tempting

  • Do not add a single SKU until move 1 has held for a quarter. Half the working range is empty; adding to it reproduces the disease.
  • The pharmacy is closed as a question — the campus hospital already runs one (owner's decision, 30 Aug 2026). The economics never worked anyway: ₹2.66 L of gross margin cannot carry a legally mandatory dedicated pharmacist at ₹2.16–3.00 L a year. What does not go away: ARY itself still sells 45 medicine SKUs without a licence — ₹39,672 a year, including one prescription-only drug. The clean fix is to stop selling them and let the hospital pharmacy handle it.
  • Do not adopt canteen-style cost-plus pricing. It would destroy roughly ₹1.08 crore of gross profit. Its premise — that ARY is ₹2.70 Cr in the hole — is a single opening-balance line dated 1 April 2023, the only entry that account has ever carried.
  • Do not run a dead-tail liquidation. Of the 13,278 active SKUs that sold nothing in twelve months, 12,904 — 97% — hold no stock at all. They are empty catalogue rows tying up no goods and no capital. Real dead stock is 374 lines worth ₹10.06 lakh, not the crores quoted. De-list them as hygiene, in hours, not as a capital release.
  • Do not chase the "₹23 lakh" of unclaimed input credit without re-deriving it first. The verification pass put the real tax booked on every GSTIN-less supplier across twelve months at ₹1,771, most of the base being nil-rated fresh produce and milk. That figure is from the verify pass, not re-measured here — but it is three orders of magnitude below the claim, so test it before funding any work.
  • Do not cut perishables in the winter window. They hold up best — dry fruit at 82.6% of term, vegetables 66.3%, fruit 59.2%. What collapses is confectionery (23.2%), bakery (18.4%) and stationery (20.2%). The published instruction would have stranded the households who stay.
  • Do not de-list the twelve "dead" Glucose SKUs by name. Dabur Glucose-D sells ₹11,591 and sold the day before this audit. De-list the discontinued brand's listings only.
  • Do not sum anything on this page into a headline number. That is how ₹13.3 crore of recommendations appeared against a ₹7.66 crore business.
07 · Still open

Seven questions no query can answer

Each costs a conversation. Several move the answer by more than any further analysis will.

Gating the moves above

  1. Who owns replenishment? Move 1 has no owner today, and that is the actual reason the shelf is empty.
  2. Does the hospital pharmacy sell devices to residents — thermometers, BP monitors, glucometers? If yes, the ₹4.3 lakh device gap closes too and there is nothing left to add.
  3. Which winter lines get marked down, and how far? Move 2 cannot execute without it.
  4. Does Akal Hospital's dispensary already retail to residents? Moves the device case ±50%.

Gating the numbers

  1. The Baru Sahib academic calendar. Never obtained. The seasonality work used a plains CBSE calendar showing a nine-day break to explain a 23–30 day trough.
  2. Nothing further on the Punjab counter — out of scope by the owner's decision. It is excluded from every figure on this page.
  3. Is each till a separately licensable FSSAI premises? The one genuinely new licensing idea, and it rests on an untested assumption.
08 · Method

How this was checked, and what it still cannot tell you

Method. Forty-six categories were researched blind — without sight of ARY's catalogue — then matched line-by-line against the live database, then rupee-sized, then adversarially attacked by an independent pass whose instruction was to refute and to default to REFUTED when a figure could not be reproduced. Thirty-eight agents; 293 claims tested; 106 refuted. Every figure on this page was pulled live from FR8HODBNEW on 30 August 2026 and carries its query in assort/vault/05-corrections/Verify-Pass-2026-08-30.md.

What is soft. The ₹4–9 lakh recoverable in move 1 is estimated — the data cannot distinguish a lost sale from one that substituted to a sibling SKU. The resident headcount of ~4,700 is a stated figure that this review's own verification weakened (it was derived by reading a rupee ratio as a headcount ratio) and it still sits underneath every per-resident number here. Twenty-five of the forty-six researched categories have not been rupee-sized; on the evidence of the twenty-one that were, they are unlikely to change the conclusion.

What was fixed to produce this. A stock-valuation defect in the toolkit priced every unit at the dearest warehouse row's cost — it read the book at ₹30.78 L against a true ₹96.90 L and turned −₹13.97 L of negative stock into −₹182.16 L. It has been corrected. A separate report that the catalogue probe was unreliable was tested and did not reproduce: five identical runs returned identical results.
