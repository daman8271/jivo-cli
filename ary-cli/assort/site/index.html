The shop is not the opportunity

ARY is the only shop for about 5,000 people who live on a closed 450-acre campus. The brief was to find every product those people need and ARY does not sell — so we started from what a life consumes, not from the shelf. Fifty-seven categories later the question had turned: ARY already stocks a whole city's worth of SKUs and cannot keep two-thirds of them on the shelf, while ₹3.06 lakh a year goes out the door on 27 lines sold below what ARY paid for them.

01 · How we are thinking about this

Start from the life, not from the shelf

Every method choice below exists to defeat one failure: if you start from what ARY already stocks, you only ever rediscover what ARY already stocks.

A shop that serves a town competes. A shop that serves a closed campus does not — so the question is not "what are we losing to a rival" but "what does a human life at Baru Sahib consume, and can that person get it here?"

That reframes the unit of analysis. Not departments, not the supplier's catalogue, not what sold last year — but the aspects of a life: eating, staying healthy, keeping a body clean, running a home, dressing for a Himachal winter, studying, praying, ageing, getting to town. Each of those consumes things. Enumerate them and you have the demand side, independent of anything ARY happens to sell.

Then, and only then, look at the shelf.

Enumerate what a life needs — blind

Break a resident's life into categories and research each one against Indian modern trade and q-commerce, without ever looking at ARY's catalogue. Each category yields SKU lines — buying decisions like "toothpaste, herbal, 100–150 g", not barcodes — tagged must-have / should-have / nice-to-have, with the cohort that buys them.

36 categories researched · 1,984 SKU lines · 860 must-have

Ask the database what ARY actually has — by name, not by tag

ARY's own category tree cannot answer this. Loose Milk is filed under Mini Meals, curd under Others, khoya under Confectionery. The "Mobile" tag understated mobile accessories by 37×. So every check searches product names across all 21,466 SKUs, ignoring the tree entirely.

ary assort probe paracetamol crocin dolo → 3 SKUs, 1 sold, ₹502/yr

Give every line one of three honest verdicts

Solid — the line's own qualifiers appear together in a real product name, and it sells. Weak — only the commodity word matched, so ARY is in the line but this pack, grade or brand is unproven. Missing — nothing matches, either way. Only solid counts toward coverage; otherwise every food category reads as 100% covered and the exercise is worthless.

medical-devices: 6 solid · 43 weak · 7 dormant · 2 missing → 11.5%

Judge the range against an outside yardstick

Coverage alone says nothing about depth. BigBasket's live metro SKU counts per category, pro-rated to 5,000 people, give a benchmark line count — then compare it against what ARY lists and what ARY actually sells. Those two numbers diverge violently, and the divergence is the whole finding.

8,000 benchmark · 14,344 listed · 4,707 selling → lists 1.79×, sells 0.59×

Cost it off the purchase ledger, never the price master

ARY's price master carries a cost higher than reality on every leaking row, so a margin report built from it can never show a loss-making line. Real cost comes from what ARY actually paid on the purchase documents. That single choice is what surfaced ₹3.06 lakh a year going out the door.

Loose Milk — master says ₹65.00 · really paid ₹51.98 · sold ₹47.00

Label every number, and keep what we got wrong

VERIFIED means a query was run and it is recorded. ESTIMATED carries its assumption. NOT-CHECKED says so. Five findings in this review were confidently wrong before the data refuted them, and all five are kept below — because the pattern is the lesson: in this database the burden of proof sits on "missing", never on "covered".

46 recorded sections · every figure carries its query or its assumption

And the method has already failed once, in a way worth stating up front. The first taxonomy had 46 categories, and it was built by looking at what Indian q-commerce carries. That is exactly the trap it was meant to avoid — just one level up. Aiming at BigBasket produced BigBasket's idea of a life, and missed eleven categories that a Sikh residential campus in the hills obviously needs. Two of them turn real money today; one of them, at a Sikh academy, is a hard zero. They are marked in the next section.

The ledger shows Akal Dairy, Akal Bakery, Akal Modi Khana (the langar provision store) and Akal Catering Mess as creditors — the campus runs its own food units, and ARY buys from them. All four relationships opened in June 2026 and are live to the last day of data: 251 vouchers, ₹4.07 lakh in one quarter. Eternal University's ₹2.51 Cr very likely flows largely to those in-house units, not to an outside market ARY could win. The realistic prize is better than "displace the mess", and more achievable. ARY has just spent a quarter becoming these units' customer, which is a working relationship to build on. Two things follow. Two-way trade: ARY buys their dairy and bread for retail while they buy ARY's staples, spices, oil and vegetables — ARY already runs the purchasing muscle (₹6.36 Cr of purchases, ten HP suppliers, a daily milk route) that four small in-house units cannot each replicate. And procurement consolidation: the real waste is that Akal Academy places 2,055 petty bills at ₹1,299 and the Trust 1,045 at ₹969 — thousands of small purchases one supply contract could absorb at a better price for the Trust and a better margin for ARY.

And it needs no assortment work whatsoever. The 59-line basket ARY already supplies to one feeding programme is a mess provisions basket: rice, atta, loose milk, dal, ghee, Fortune oil, tea, sugar, spices, vegetables. Every line is already in the catalogue. A bulk pricing tier already exists too — 65 SKUs carry _Z/_L suffixes and turn ₹39.9 lakh a year. The channel is built. It serves one customer.

There is no pharmacy, and now it is measured rather than assumed verified

Across 21,466 SKUs, ARY holds three paracetamol lines. One sold: Crocin 500 mg, 26 strips, ₹502.06 in twelve months, last on 14 February 2026. Antacid, antibiotic and every chronic-disease medicine return a hard zero — absent from the catalogue, not merely filed elsewhere.

A township of 5,000, several thousand of them boarding children, bought 26 strips of paracetamol in a year. The "Medicare" group's ₹3.15 lakh is Dettol, Eno, Vicks, Boroline and digestive churan — a home-remedy shelf, not a dispensary. JIVO's own Pain Relief Oil is its second-best seller.

Two things must be settled before any money moves. First, ProductChildMaster has 111,021 rows and zero populated expiry or manufacture dates — FusionERP8 as configured cannot hold a pharmaceutical batch, and no pharmacy can legally operate without one. That is a software question, and it is the real blocker. Second, Akal Charitable Hospital is on the same campus — 100 beds, 11 doctors, ~120 free OPD patients a day. It likely already holds a licence, and whatever it dispenses free is demand a retail pharmacy will never see. One internal conversation answers both.

The real diagnosis: ARY has an availability problem, not an assortment problem verified five ways

Adding thousands more SKUs to a catalogue where 68% of the existing ones never sell would make the problem worse, not better.

The work is the opposite of the brief: order what is already listed, delete what will never sell, and add narrow new ranges one at a time. Which is exactly what ARY's own two successes did.

One correction this forces on this report. §07 records that a baby aisle would be dead stock, because ARY sells only 425 diaper packs a year. That still holds on volume — but a parent needing a month of nappies cannot buy them at ARY at any price, since the monthly pack has never been ordered. Small infant base and unstocked bulk packs produce an identical signal. The cheap test is to order the two existing monthly-pack codes once — ₹5,000 of stock settles a question no further analysis can.

Delete ~5,400 dead lines, add ~1,500 real ones. The catalogue shrinks and the revenue grows — the opposite of carrying every product.

Caution on the numbers. The metro counts are verified but the pro-rata is an estimate, and BigBasket's category tree does not map cleanly onto ARY's (§07 — ARY's tree is unreliable, so the "listed" column inherits that noise). The direction and magnitude are sound: 4× over-listed in beauty and 10× under-sold in dairy are far too large to be mapping error. Re-derive any single category before acting on its exact figure.

Three categories are on the books and have never once sold verified

Medical devices (3 SKUs), hand tools (18 listed, 8 active) and LED/battery (47 SKUs, 5 moving) produced ₹0, ₹0 and ₹8,575. That is not assortment, it is paperwork — and it is the pattern across the catalogue: 13,284 of 19,481 active SKUs did not sell at all, while 252 SKUs earn half the revenue and 1,108 earn 80%. The dead tail locks almost no stock, so this is catalogue hygiene rather than trapped capital — but every dead line still has to be priced, counted and shelved.

Against that, sanitary pads work: ₹83 per resident per year on 81 SKUs, roughly ₹200 a year for each woman and girl on campus. The model performs when the range actually exists.

Which makes the rest of women's health the clearest underserved cohort in the business. Menstrual cups, intimate wash, pregnancy tests and tampons are all a hard zero — no product name in the catalogue contains any of those words — against ~1,102 university students (487 of them AIRWE rural women), several hundred senior schoolgirls, and resident staff women. Pads prove the demand converts when the product is on the shelf. A menstrual cup is also a ₹300–500 one-off replacing years of pads, on a residential campus with limited disposal infrastructure.

Two climate-driven gaps the hill site makes obvious. Lip balm: 6 SKUs, all six sold, ₹9,655 — a fully sold-through range that is simply too small, on a campus whose January low is 2.8 °C. Sunscreen: ₹17.68 per resident per year at 1,551 m, where UV is materially higher than the plains and students are outdoors daily. For contrast, cold cream and moisturiser already earn ₹110 per resident per year — same climate, a range that exists.

05 · Constraints the plan has to respect

Three facts that bound everything above

The population is flat to shrinking verified

Eternal University has 1,102 students — not the 2,500 on Wikipedia or the 1,300 on the Trust's own site — and had 1,108 a decade ago. It fills 51.6% of sanctioned seats and has ~400 spare hostel beds.

  • Akal Academy Grade X registrations: 125 → 98 (−22%)
  • Grade XII: 113 → 57 (−50%)
  • Term-time township total ≈ 4,550–4,800 — the 5,000 brief holds

ARY's +29% is wallet share, not more customers. Anyone sizing this campus off the public web over-forecasts the university cohort by 127%.

January empties the campus verified 3×

Measured on ARY's own till in three consecutive winters. Demand falls to a hard, flat floor for about three weeks and steps back up on a single day.

  • 2025-26: 1,067 → 376 bills/day, a 23-day plateau, 29 Dec → 20 Jan
  • Floor is 35–49% of term demand across all three years
  • Permanent resident base ≈ 1,700–2,300 people

Actionable directly: stop buying ~29 December, restart ~20 January. Peak is March–June.

Two counters were switched off with live populations behind them verified

Talwandi Sabo ran ~9,500 bills and ~₹4.8 lakh a month at its plateau, then decayed over four months and stopped on 11 July 2024. Akal University there has since grown 2,172 → 2,703 students (+24.4%).

Ary Apple A Day was Eternal University's cafeteria — ₹38.43 lakh lifetime, dark 33 months, and EU's 2024-25 annual report still names it as the campus cafeteria.

Reinstating Talwandi's prior run-rate is worth ₹17–18 lakh of gross profit a year at retail margin. But it carries 607 rows of negative book stock and ₹1.24 crore of lifetime shortage — that closure was not orderly, and it must be understood before stock goes back in.

The books need attention regardless verified

  • Physical counts show ₹2.59 Cr shortage and ₹1.67 Cr excess, net −₹91.5 lakh = 4.1% of lifetime net sales against a 1–2% norm. Both halves being large points at book-keeping, not only loss.
  • G Canteen alone is −₹40.19 lakh, consistent with recipe consumption never being posted — which also inflates every prepared-food margin.
  • 97% of bills (1,054,205 of 1,088,607) are booked to one anonymous walk-in account; SalesPersonID is empty on 99.7%.
  • A probable duplicate ₹5.91 lakh bill on 11 May 2026 — two identical 59-line invoices a minute apart, and ARY has no cancellation document.
06 · Recommendation

Ten moves, ranked by return on effort

Ordered so that each step is cheap relative to what it unlocks, and so nothing expensive happens before the question it depends on is answered.

0

Stop selling 27 SKUs below cost — starting with one

₹3.06 L/yr leaking now₹0 costHoursBlocker: none

Sweeping every SKU's 12-month sale rate against its 12-month purchase cost finds 27 lines sold below what ARY paid, worth −₹3.06 lakh a year. "Am Lower" alone is 1,512 units at −₹90.89 each: −₹1.37 lakh — the largest single leak found anywhere in this review, fixable with one price change. Loose milk is −₹66,951: the purchase cost went ₹45 → ₹55 over the year while the retail price stayed hard-coded at ₹47.00 across all 16,105 litres, with zero variance. It was invisible because the price master still carries the old ₹38.83 cost, so every margin report shows loose milk at a healthy +35%.

12 · Corrections

Six things this review got wrong before the data corrected them

Recorded because each one was plausible enough to act on, and each would have cost money.

"No diapers or formula, on a campus with married staff families"

Wrong. ARY sold 425 diaper packs in twelve months — about eight nappies a day, which is one to two children. Infant formula: 13 packs. The residential cohort is boarding children aged 5–18 and students aged 18–25, not families with infants. Baby toiletries do sell (~₹1.5 lakh), but in India adults buy those. A baby aisle would have been dead stock.

"₹1,493 of spend per resident per month"

Overstated. 14.41% of this year's revenue is nine bills to one Delhi organisation — a food charity at Green Park, New Delhi, which is not a resident. Strip the institutional accounts and the honest figure is ≈₹1,290. Any per-resident number must exclude them first.

"Dairy has 41 SKUs; Mobile has 8 SKUs and sold ₹2,690"

Both came from ARY's category tree, and the tree cannot be trusted for this question. Loose Milk is filed under Mini Meals, curd under Others, khoya under Confectionery, fresh peas under Fruits. Name-searching dairy finds ₹64.8 lakh spread across 14 groups, under 15% of it inside "Dairy Products".

Mobile accessories are really 14 SKUs doing ₹99,713 across 3 groups — the group tag was wrong by 37×. This is why ary assort probe was built, and why every "missing category" claim must be re-tested with it before stock is bought.

"LPG is a ₹4.80 lakh business at ARY, never researched"

Wrong, and wrong twice over. There is no LPG business. The figure was a name-match false positive: "Lmk" is an ARY bakery brand (Lmk Pts = pastries at ₹9, Lmk Bc Vn = birthday cake at ₹350), and the term "gas" caught Gas Stove and Gas Lighter — cookware.

The one real LPG item, Lmk Gas, is filed under Cooking Consumable at ₹2,200 a cylinder and has sold 10 units in the life of the database, ₹27,003 all-time. It is the canteens' own cooking fuel held as stock.

This was the fifth false positive from name-matching in this review — after arhar dal, almonds, washing bars and the mobile group tag. The pattern is now unambiguous: every probe result must be read for contamination before it is quoted, and a term that could be a brand prefix or a compound word is not evidence of anything.

81 "must-have SKU lines ARY has nothing for" — a tooling failure, caught before it shipped

The automated coverage diff produced a clean, plausible list of 81 missing must-have lines. Five spot-checks against the live catalogue killed it: it called arhar dal missing (ARY has 12 SKUs turning ₹71,949) and almonds missing (151 SKUs, ₹7,97,032), along with cashew, peanuts and chikki.

Cause: the diff AND-ed the distinctive words of each researched line, so "Toor / arhar dal — economy grade, sold loose" became '%toor%' AND '%arhar%' AND '%dal%' — matching nothing, because ARY's SKU is called plainly "Arhar Daal 1 Kg". The AND had been added to stop the opposite failure, where the single word milk matches 389 SKUs and marked all 52 dairy lines covered. It overcorrected.

Fixed with a two-tier verdict, and the tier is now reported per line: specific (the line's own qualifiers appear together — real coverage), broad (only the commodity word matched — ARY is in the line, this pack or grade unproven, and not counted in the coverage %), or missing (nothing matches either way). In this database the default assumption must be that ARY has it under a different name — the burden of proof sits on "missing", not on "covered". Every gap in this report was verified with ary assort probe against the live catalogue, not taken from the diff.

"₹31,200 of Schedule H drugs sold without a licence" unverified lane

Overstated by a research lane whose adversarial check was cut short. The largest lines — Vicks VapoRub, Inhaler and BabyRub, ₹28,100 — are licensed in India as Ayurvedic proprietary medicines, not Schedule H. The genuinely prescription molecules are Benadryl, Volini/Omnigel diclofenac, Crocin and Disprin: ≈₹25,000 a year.

The legal point underneath it does stand — India has no statutory OTC category, so a compliant "OTC-only corner" is not a thing that exists. But this is ₹25,000 of exposure in a ₹9 crore business, and it is what every kirana in India does. A housekeeping item for the pharmacy decision, not a scandal.

13 · The instrument

This is now a command, not a one-off study

ARY already had a read-only CLI. It gained an assort module — twelve commands that answer the expansion question on live data, so this brief can be re-derived any week rather than re-commissioned. 

# what we have — live SQL, always current
ary assort probe paracetamol crocin dolo    # does ARY carry this, wherever it's filed?
ary assort coverage                          # per category: listed vs moving, ₹/resident/yr
ary assort velocity                          # 252 SKUs = 50% of revenue; 13,284 = none
ary assort wallet                            # capture per resident, by counter and category
ary assort headroom                          # turns, dead range, shelf to reclaim
ary assort dead --by-group                   # the listing tail and what it ties up
ary assort taxonomy --subgroups              # the category tree as the operator built it
ary assort benchmark                         # capture rate vs real household spend

# what people need — the research corpus
ary assort research                          # what's researched, diffed, ranked
ary assort gaps --state missing              # SKU lines ARY has nothing for
ary assort priority --p P0                   # build order, sized in ₹/month
ary assort sweep --lane population           # findings, each tagged VERIFIED or ESTIMATED

Read-only by construction

Every statement passes a single-SELECT guard and runs inside a transaction that is always rolled back. Proven refused: UPDATE, DELETE, DROP, INSERT, TRUNCATE, EXEC, xp_cmdshell, and a stacked SELECT 1; DROP TABLE.

Across 218 research agents, zero sapb1 invocations and no write to any JIVO system — checked against every agent transcript and the shared write logs.

Provenance is built in

probe exists because coverage was proven unreliable for the question people actually ask. benchmark refuses to run without a sourced benchmark file rather than inventing one. Every corpus finding carries VERIFIED / ESTIMATED / NOT-CHECKED, and every priority figure carries the penetration assumption it was built from, so it can be argued with.
