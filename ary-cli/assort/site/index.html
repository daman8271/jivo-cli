The empty shelf

Five thousand people live on a closed campus. One shop serves them, and there is nowhere else to go. Half of everything that shop sold this year is out of stock today.

The place

One shop, one road, five thousand people

Baru Sahib is a 450-acre township — a residential school, a university, a hospital, a de-addiction centre. The nearest real market is 30 to 50 km of hill road away.

It opened 363 days out of 364. A shop that closes is a shop people plan around; this one is simply always there. That is the whole basis of the opportunity — and the whole weight of the obligation.

The main counter takes 63% of the money. Clothing takes 19%. The canteen, the fruit-and-vegetable counter and a small second shop take the rest.

The question

What does a life actually need?

We did not start from the shelf. A shop's own sales can only ever tell you about things it already stocks — the gap is invisible there by definition. So we started from the life, and asked what it consumes.

50 categories in scope a hard zero at ARY ruled out — ethics or practicality

Fifty-seven categories. Nine are ruled out and must never be counted as gaps: eggs, meat and fish on ethics — this is a vegetarian Sikh institution, and it is not open to revisiting — along with tobacco and alcohol; LPG, because a gas agency is an oil-company distributorship and the campus already has a station; vehicles; newspapers, which already arrive daily; and laundry and furnishing, which stay on the shelf but out of scope.

That leaves fifty. We researched what each should contain against Indian modern trade — 1,984 buying decisions across 46 categories — deliberately without looking at ARY's own catalogue, so the list could not simply reproduce the shelf it was meant to test.

Method

How the answer was arrived at

Enumerate what a life needs, blind to the shelf

46 categories researched against Indian modern trade and quick-commerce, without once looking at ARY's catalogue. 1,984 buying decisions, tagged must-have or should-have, with the cohort that buys them.

Ask the database by name, never by its own category tag

ARY files loose milk under "Mini Meals" and curd under "Others". The "Mobile" tag understated mobile accessories by 37×. So every check searches product names across all 21,479 SKUs and ignores the tree.

Cost it off the purchase ledger, never the price list

ARY's price master shows a cost higher than reality on most loss-making lines, so a margin report built from it can never show a loss. Real cost is what ARY actually paid.

Attack every finding before publishing it

Each result was handed to a second reviewer whose only job was to refute it. 106 of 293 claims broke. Those are recorded at the end of this page rather than quietly removed.

The question changed once the numbers came back.

This is not a range problem. It is a reordering problem.

That 94% is the number the whole argument turns on. It separates failed to reorder from deliberately dropped. Two of the 895 had never been bought at all; the rest were on order books an average of six months ago.

Those lines earned ₹19.52 lakh of gross margin at 24.1% when they were on the shelf. Getting some of that back needs no new category, no licence and no capital — only a reorder point. Not one product in ARY's catalogue has one set.

What is genuinely absent

And then there is the pharmacy that isn't

Availability explains most of the gap. It does not explain all of it. A few things are simply not there, and were never there.

No metformin, no telmisartan, no amlodipine, no atorvastatin, no thyroxine — not under any brand name, not under any spelling. No glucometer, no blood-pressure monitor, no pregnancy test, no reading glasses. On a campus with a hospital, a de-addiction centre and several thousand boarding children.

The blocker is not the licence. ARY's system has never once recorded a real expiry date — the field exists and 26 rows of 111,253 carry a value, all of them from 2018. No pharmacy can operate without batch and expiry tracking. That is a software question, and it has to be answered before any money is spent.

Twelve wholesale bills worth ₹70 lakh returned ₹80,566. Any plan to grow that channel is a plan to grow the one that earns nothing — and the earlier version of this report recommended exactly that.

Separately, 24 products are sold for less than ARY paid for them. Loose milk is the largest: bought at ₹55 a litre, sold at a flat ₹47 across every transaction for nine months. Not one of those litres went to a campus resident — all of it went to the wholesale account.

What to do

Four moves, and three cost nothing

The old plan was to add categories. Half the working range being empty makes every "add" a recommendation to buy stock for shelves nobody refills.

Put reorder points on the 895 empty regulars

₹81 lakh of proven repeat demand exposed, ₹19.5 lakh of gross margin it earned when it was on shelf. No capital, no licence, no new SKU. Nothing else starts until this holds a quarter.

Stop the 24 below-cost lines

Hours of work, no cost. Fix the loose-milk price, and decide whether the wholesale account is worth serving at 1.1%.

Settle the compliance questions

Free to ask. Whether ARY may sell what it is already selling, and whether the system can hold an expiry date at all — the second decides the pharmacy.

Only then, add — narrowly

Both things that ever worked here were narrow ranges kept in stock: the fruit-and-vegetable counter turns its stock 172 times a year on about 55 products. Add one category a quarter, funded by deleting dead lines, never ten at once.

What we got wrong

106 of 293 claims did not survive

Every one failed the same way: a matcher or an aggregate that looked authoritative and was not. They are here because a report that hides its errors should not be believed about anything else.

"The campus mess spends ₹2.51 crore a year and ARY has 1.1% of it — a ₹4–5 crore opportunity."

Eternal University bought ₹99,484 from ARY last year. The ₹2.51 crore was cooked-meal transfer charges plus staff boarding offset by staff collections — both sides of the ratio were the wrong thing.

And the campus runs its own dairy, bakery and provision store, which ARY buys from.

"₹1.07 crore of dead stock sits in a 13,400-line tail."

Real dead stock is ₹10.59 lakh. Of the 15,249 lines, 14,853 hold no stock at all — they are empty catalogue rows, not inventory.

The valuation multiplied a quantity sum by the dearest cost ever recorded. It priced 21,287 buttons at ₹40.45 lakh; row by row they are worth ₹5,256.

"Identical footfall, different shelf, 25% less spend."

Withdrawn. The best-stocked days carried 715 bills against 484 — 48% more traffic, not identical. Hold the month constant and the effect is 46 paise a bill, indistinguishable from zero.

The best-stocked stretch is September to December, which is term time. It was measuring the academic calendar.

"The shop sold 26 strips of paracetamol all year."

85. ARY spells it Paracitamol, so the search found nothing and we reported the gap as worse than it is.

Same class of error as calling arhar dal and almonds missing while the shop was selling ₹8.7 lakh of them.

"Milk sold below cost may be a deliberate subsidy for boarding children."

Not one litre reached a campus resident. All of it went to the Delhi wholesale account.

A charitable reading of a number nobody had traced to its buyer.

"Three in four products are out of stock."

51.2%. The higher figure counted price-revision records rather than products — there are 3.17 of them per product, and 55% of the "empty" ones belonged to products that were in stock.

Caught in review, before publication. The arithmetic was flawless; the unit was wrong.

Method. Every figure was read live from ARY's database on 30–31 August 2026 and carries the query that produced it. Figures are retail — the Delhi wholesale account is excluded from anything described as retail. Stock is measured at product level across all warehouses, on-hand at 30 August 2026.

Limits. The 5,000 headcount is a stated figure, not a queried one. Category coverage percentages are not published here: the underlying matcher marked six categories 100% complete, which is a permissive matcher rather than a finding. Gross margin is computed on purchase-ledger weighted-average cost, and 16% of sales carry no cost record at all.

Nothing in this analysis wrote to any system. The tool used is read-only by construction — every statement passes a select-only guard inside a transaction that is always rolled back.
