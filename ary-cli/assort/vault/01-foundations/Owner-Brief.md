# Owner brief — what ARY is actually trying to do

> **This note outranks any inference elsewhere in the vault.** Where a finding assumed
> something about intent, cohorts or campus structure, this is the correction.

## The ask, in one line

**ARY has a monopoly on 5,000 people. The owner wants everything those people need to be
buyable at ARY** — so that nobody has to arrange anything elsewhere. Not a margin play
first; a completeness play.

> "The owner basically said that we need literally everything."

## The method the owner's side is running

Six steps, and they are sound. This vault was built along the same line:

1. Capture what 5,000 people who live together actually need, **by cohort** — students,
   staff, elders ([[Population]])
2. Turn that into **categories** of daily life — see the 57-category taxonomy
3. For each category, list what ARY already has ([[Gap-List]])
4. **Sub-categorise: what are the genuinely best products people use in real life
   outside** — this is the demand research, done blind
5. Compare against ARY's shelf, produce the add list
6. **Add on a priority basis, as a pilot.** Small money, not heavy investment. Measure in
   1-2 months and let the results decide

**What step 7 must be, and currently cannot be:** *did it work?* ARY cannot answer that
today — 96% of bills are anonymous, so a shelf change moves one aggregate number and
nothing else. See [[Digital-Identity]].

## The shelf-replacement arithmetic the owner asked for

Active SKUs banded by how often they actually sell, 12 months:

| Band | SKUs | 12m sales | % of revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20+ bills/month — stars | **828** | ₹3.84 Cr | **46.9%** |
| 4-20 bills/month — workers | 1,411 | ₹2.06 Cr | 25.2% |
| 1-4 bills/month — slow | 1,686 | ₹1.21 Cr | 14.8% |
| under 1 bill/month — stale | 2,455 | ₹1.07 Cr | 13.1% |
| **never sold in 12m** | **13,101** | **₹0** | **0.00%** |

**2,239 SKUs earn 72% of revenue. 13,101 earn nothing.**

Two conclusions:

- **Shelf space is not the constraint.** 13,101 lines can go tomorrow at zero revenue cost.
  The real constraints are operator attention (someone must price, count and reorder every
  line) and working capital per line.
- **The bar a new line must clear is ₹14,600-46,300 a year.** That sizes the pilot: 500 new
  lines at ₹15,000 = ₹75 lakh of potential revenue, a 10% lift. Assume half land →
  ~₹37 lakh revenue, ~₹11 lakh gross profit at the 31% retail rate, on maybe ₹8-10 lakh of
  opening stock. Small, which matches the owner's instruction not to invest heavily.

## Corrections to earlier assumptions in this vault

| Earlier assumption | Correction from the owner's side |
|---|---|
| Elderly cohort worth weighting | **There are not many elderly.** Scale the elderly/mobility range right down — cover it "to some extent, not to that extent" |
| Pet care ruled out | **Pet food is back IN.** People on campus do keep pets |
| Medical devices low priority | **Keep them.** They should be stocked |
| The campus is one institution | **It is many** — academy, university, School of Spiritual Sciences, de-addiction centre, hospital. Separate and respectful of each other, but collaborative |
| "The mess" is one buyer | **Messes differ by institution.** The academy mess and the university girls' arrangement work differently; university students can order food by phone |
| ARY competes with the campus's own units | ARY **is** the umbrella — "it is under one but there are services within the one." The bakery and dairy are the school's; ARY sells vegetables and clothing; goods move between them |
| Hospital is an outside body | **The hospital is within the group**, has its own farm/medical arm, and **orders its own medicine today.** ARY may take that on — needs a conversation first |
| Retail vs institutional unclear | **Confirmed: maximum margin is retail walk-in.** Matches the measured 31.0% vs 7.2% ([[Institutional]]) |

## What this means for priorities

The owner named the pilot categories himself as examples of where to start —
**male grooming, cosmetics, sunblock.** All three are:

- high margin (Personal Care runs 33%)
- low unit cost, so a small pilot buys real breadth
- climate- or cohort-driven at this site (sunscreen at 1,551 m is currently
  ₹17.68/resident/year — see [[Gap-List]])

That is the right shape for a first pilot: **cheap, high-margin, fast-turning, and easy to
reverse if it fails.**
