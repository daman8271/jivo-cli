## Important detail from the full transcript

### Machine changeovers and oil handling

- **Same bottle, different oil:** the early Clear Pack discussion says no mechanical
  part change is needed if the bottle remains the same; flushing is a separate
  activity (p17, 00:31-00:43). The historical summary's "same SKU" wording is not a
  reliable description of that example.
- **Clear Pack combined change:** pack-part work and oil flushing can both apply.
  The example of leaving mustard while changing pack adds flushing separately
  (p18, 01:17-02:01). The revised generic one-hour SKU entry does not eliminate
  those longer specific cases. Do not infer whether every activity can overlap.
- **JP total time:** the 5-6 hours already includes approximately 2-2.5 hours
  cleaning and all flushing (pp27-28, 10:04-10:31). Adding cleaning or flushing
  again would double-count the delay. Bottle design and neck differences can
  require changes even when both products are one-litre packs (p27, 09:29-09:41).
- **10 Head total:** transcript wording fluctuates between one hour and one and a
  half hours, and between flushing-only and combined time (pp32-33, 14:44-15:33).
  Adopt the revised note's combined one-hour-plus entry. Do not add a further
  30-minute parts allowance to that combined entry.
- **6 Head:** inherits the reported 10 Head changeover description; manual
  labelling is the stated difference (pp36-37, 20:42-21:31). That does not establish
  a measured per-pack labelling speed or a fixed additional batch delay.
- **Flushing disposition:** the historical speakers say oil is mixed and stored
  separately, not simply thrown away, but are unsure of its eventual destination
  or reuse (pp28-29, 10:33-11:14). The current user assumption settles reuse for
  the plan; this conversation does not supply a recovery percentage.
- Later draining discussion describes emptying line storage, connecting a pipe,
  manual draining into a drum and transfer/closure (p59, 53:28-54:03). No usable
  extra duration or commercial drum-filling capacity is given.

### Routes, speeds and hardware

- **Clear Pack:** revised 1/4/5 L routes control. The transcript explicitly rejects

  "only 1 and 5 L" summary wording is superseded.
- **JP:** repeated late instruction settles 75/min, with vibration mentioned at
  90/min (pp47-48, 33:48-35:17). Preserve 200 ml as occasional from the revised
  note. Do not use 4,500/hour as a 200 ml rate. A bottle-design change-part set is

  22:01-22:26); exact tooling and item codes remain absent.
- **10 Head:** statements that the filler can handle larger packs are followed
  by explicit operational exclusion because larger labels do not fit. The late
  instruction removes 3/4/5 L; use 1/2 L (pp43-45, 29:11-31:02). Bottle material
  and label dimensions matter; a filled but unlabelled product is not proof of a
  complete usable production route.
- **6 Head:** mainly 3/4/5 L, 4 L sometimes; 1 L only for emergencies when another
  line has a problem (pp45-47, 31:40-33:13). The adopted 3/4/5 L rates are

- **Tin machine:** the original speech confuses "three head" with "three
  machines", and mixes 500, 8 and 10 with uncertain units (pp49-51, 36:06-38:14).
  The revised note identifies the 15 L tin machine at 10/min. Do not invent a

- **Pouches:** Hitech 20/min; Samarpan 30-35/min. The machines can run together or

- **Manual filling:** exists and is counted in software, but has no fixed speed

### Running losses, bottles, people and QC

- **Running speed versus elapsed output:** the transcript explicitly describes
  interruptions for sticker changes, jams, torn labels and breakdowns (p31,
  13:20-13:28). The historical 45-tonne versus 30-tonne claim is not a verified
  full-shift dataset or an efficiency factor. Do not derive a planning multiplier
  from it, or infer shift hours from the separate "divide by 11" remark.
- **Clear Pack bottle faults:** the tour describes a dented 40 g bottle jamming,
  spilling oil and requiring cleaning, with examples around 5-6 or 10 minutes
  (pp54-55, 43:54-45:30). Two extra people are mentioned for label problems.
  These are incident examples, not an average downtime or failure-frequency rule.
- **JP bottle faults:** 26 g bottles arrive on an air conveyor; slight dents can
  jam and cause overflow (p56, 46:30-46:55). Later cleanup examples mention 10,
  20 and 25-30 minutes (p56, 47:23-47:56). They are not additive fixed penalties.
- **Blowing and power:** dent problems are also linked to blowing/heating quality
  and interrupted electricity (pp57-58, 49:52-50:43). The suggestion to increase
  a bottle from 40 g to 50 g is not a completed packaging-specification change or
  proof that weight is the sole cause.
- **Labour:** four workers are described applying 6 Head labels manually; later
  references mention one or two rework workers (pp59-60, 52:33-54:48). Do not
  sum potentially overlapping counts or call them the full line crew. The revised
  note's wording is "4 + labour people".
- **Inline QC:** inspectors set defective bottles aside while the line continues
  (p60, 55:21-55:44). This does not determine incoming-material QC, batch release,
  startup clearance, final stock readiness or their respective durations.

- **Space:** the source note strikes out "Space is a issue and a bottleneck".
