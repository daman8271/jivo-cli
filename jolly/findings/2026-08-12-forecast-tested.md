# Finding 09 — the forecast tested, and where the oil is actually going

**Date:** 2026-08-12 · Jivo Oil · read-only
**Supersedes Finding 07's claim that the August plan is "a wish".**

Operator confirmed the 4,241 MT August plan is a **forecast**, not a target. That
makes it testable, so I tested it. It holds up. My earlier reading was wrong.

---

## 1. Why the earlier test was wrong

Finding 07 compared a **full-month forecast** (4,241 MT) against a **full-month
actual** (July, 2,344 MT net) and concluded the plan was 1.8× reality.

That is the wrong comparison. On 12 August the month is a third gone, and the
question is not "is the forecast bigger than last month" — it is "is this month
tracking toward it". The right test is day-matched: the same first 12 days of
every prior month.

| **2026-08** | **932,667** | 229,773 | **1,162,440** |

**August's first twelve days are larger than any full first-twelve-days in the
fourteen-month series — by 89%.** The previous best was 684,331 (July 2025).

Extrapolated at the current daily rate: **≈ 3,000 MT**. But every prior month is
back-loaded — the first 12 days are historically only 20–26% of the month, not the
39% a flat extrapolation assumes. On the historical shape, August lands somewhere
around **3,500–4,000 MT**.

Against a 4,241 MT forecast, that is close. **Slightly ambitious at worst, credible,
and nothing like "a wish".**

Confidence: medium-high on the direction, medium on the landing number. Twelve days
is twelve days, and the month-fraction ratio has ranged from 9% to 29% historically,
so the extrapolation band is genuinely wide.

## 3. The whole surge is one channel: Oil → Mart

| First 12 days | Interco | vs July |
|---|---:|---|
| 2026-07 | 243,984 | — |
| **2026-08** | **932,667** | **3.8×** |

**932,667 L of August's 1,162,440 L — 80% — is `CUSTA000606 JIVO MART PVT LTD`,
across 52 invoices.** Not one big order; a sustained daily flow.

That is 2.3× the previous intercompany record (399,936, May 2026) and 3.8× July's
same period. This is a real step-change, not a billing artefact.

**And it lines up with the forecast's own structure.** The plan carries 2,343 MT of
"Ecom Planning" — and e-commerce sells through Mart, not Oil. So the Oil→Mart
transfer *is* the e-com channel filling. Running the interco rate out to month-end
gives ≈ 2,400 MT, against a 2,343 MT ecom forecast. Those two numbers agreeing is
the strongest evidence the forecast is sound.

### External sales are soft, but not alarming

External is 229,773 in the first 12 days against July's 370,621 — down 38%. But the
series runs 79k–391k, so August sits mid-range, and external billing is the part
that back-loads hardest. **Not a red flag yet. Worth watching, and cheap to watch —
if external is still below 300k by the 20th, something real is happening in trade.**

## 4. What this changes

The pressure on raw material is not a general demand rise. **It is e-commerce,
pulling through Mart, at roughly four times its recent rate.** Everything in
Finding 08 — mustard oil short against stock, PET bottles short against stock, lines
running at 30% performance — is downstream of that one channel stepping up.

Two consequences for the pilot:

1. **The e-com/Mart demand signal is the leading indicator, and Oil cannot see it.**
   Oil only learns about it when Mart raises a transfer order. By then the oil has
   to already be in the tank. A separate Mart system is planned; until it exists,
   the Oil pilot is forecasting from a shadow.
2. **The allocation question is now concrete.** If ~2,400 MT of the month is going
   to one internal customer, then the trade channel is competing for what's left.
   Deciding that split deliberately — rather than by whoever's order lands first —
   is the highest-value thing this system can do.

## 5. Correction, stated plainly

Finding 07 called the plan "not a plan, a wish". That was wrong, and wrong in a way
worth naming: I compared a forecast to a different period's actual, got a ratio that
looked alarming, and wrote it up with more confidence than a single ratio earns. The
day-matched test was cheap and I should have run it before writing the conclusion,
not after being asked.
