Smart Supply Chain System
An Intelligent, Alarm-Driven Platform — from Planning to Dispatch
Management Brief

1.  Executive Summary

Today, five departments drive our supply chain independently — packaging-material procurement, oil and raw-material procurement, finance, infrastructure (storage and filling-line capacity), and production. Each is led by its own HOD, each keeps its own view of the numbers, and coordination between them is manual, slow, and reactive.
This brief proposes a single, intelligent Supply Chain System that unifies these functions into one shared, real-time view. It does not merely display data — it senses what needs to happen, calculates the true requirement for every SKU, and raises alarms early enough for each department to act before a shortfall stops the line.
The result: one HOD supported by a small team can steer the entire chain from monthly plan to dispatch, with faster and better-informed decisions, and far less firefighting.
2.  The Problem Today

	•	Siloed information — planning, procurement, production and dispatch each live in separate sheets and systems, so no one sees the full picture at once.
	•	Reactive, not proactive — shortfalls in packaging material or oil are usually discovered late, when production is already at risk, forcing rushed and costly purchases.
	•	HOD overload — every HOD spends time manually reconciling plan versus stock versus capacity — effort that repeats every cycle.
	•	No early warning — with no lead-time-aware alerts, we cannot reliably tell what must be ordered today versus what can wait.
	•	Inconsistent buffers — safety-stock levels are not consistently enforced across finished goods and packaging, so buffers erode unnoticed.
3.  The Proposed Solution

An interactive, alarm-driven dashboard that acts as the single brain of the supply chain. It continuously combines our live ERP data with fixed reference data from each department, and turns the monthly plan into a time-phased, department-wise action list.
Core capabilities:
	•	Senses requirements — computes exactly what to produce, per SKU, from plan and demand.
	•	Checks availability — checks packaging and raw material on hand against what production will consume.
	•	Alarms early — flags what to procure immediately versus what can wait, based on each material's lead time.
	•	Respects capacity — verifies the plan is achievable against real machine capacity, not just on paper.
	•	Fully interactive — drill into any SKU, department, or material and get the answer instantly.
4.  How It Works — Plan to Dispatch

The system runs a single, logical chain for every SKU:
	•	Demand signal — combine the monthly planning sheet with the last three months of actual sales to establish true demand.
	•	Stock floor — maintain a minimum of 35% of the three-month sales trend as safety stock, per SKU, for both finished goods and packaging materials.
	•	Finished-goods gap — compare warehouse stock against demand plus the 35% floor; the shortfall is what we must produce.
	•	BOM explosion — explode each finished good (parent) into its child items — packaging and raw materials — using the live Bill of Materials from the ERP.
	•	Material requirement — subtract packaging stock on hand and its own 35% floor; what remains is the true procurement requirement.
	•	Timing — apply lead times so each department knows what to order now versus later.
	•	Feasibility — check machine capacity and the material-to-machine map to confirm production is realistic, through to dispatch.

6.  Benefits to the Business

	•	Fewer stockouts — procurement acts on lead-time-based alarms instead of last-minute crises.
	•	Protected buffers — consistent 35% buffers on finished goods and packaging, enforced automatically.
	•	Lower HOD load — one shared view replaces repeated manual reconciliation across five teams.
	•	Faster decisions — decisions are driven by live, exploded requirements — not by memory or guesswork.
	•	Cost control — early visibility of true requirements curbs rush purchases and idle inventory.
	•	One source of truth — plan, stock, production and dispatch stay aligned end to end.
7.  What We Need to Proceed

	•	Reference data — Department sign-off to provide the reference data (lead times, machine capacities, material-to-machine mapping) via the prepared template.
	•	System access — Approved access to pull sales, stock and BOM from the ERP for the dashboard.
	•	Ownership — Confirmation of the single owning HOD and the supporting team of up to five.
